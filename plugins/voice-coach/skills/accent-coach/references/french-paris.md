# French (Parisian): Sound Guide

## Core target sounds

**Vowels**
- Front rounded vowels: /y/ (*tu*, *rue*): say /i/ and round the lips tightly without moving the tongue. /ø/ (*deux*, *peu*) and /œ/ (*peur*, *seul*): round /e/ and /ɛ/.
- /u/ (*ou*, *vous*): lips rounded, tongue back. Do not diphthongize: no glide at the end.
- /e/ vs /ɛ/ (*été* vs *était*) and /o/ vs /ɔ/ (*beau* vs *bol*): closed vs open.
- Nasal vowels: /ɑ̃/ (*an, en*), /ɔ̃/ (*on*), /ɛ̃/ (*in, ain*) (and /œ̃/ (*un*) is merged with /ɛ̃/ for many Parisian speakers). Do not pronounce the N: air flows through the nose, the tongue does not touch the roof of the mouth.
- /ə/ (*e muet*, *le*, *de*): lips slightly rounded, often dropped in rapid speech (*samedi* ≈ "sam'di").
- Pure vowels: French vowels do not glide. *é* is /e/, not "ay".

**Consonants**
- /ʁ/: uvular R, a soft friction in the throat. Start from /g/ or /k/ and relax the contact. It is voiced after voiced sounds and may devoice at the end of words (*pour* → /puʁ/).
- /p t k/ are **not aspirated**, and /t d/ are dental (tongue tip against upper teeth).
- /ɥ/ (*nuit*, *huit*), /ɲ/ (*agneau*), /ʒ/ (*je*) and /ʃ/ (*chat*).
- Final consonants are often silent, except C, R, F, L (the "CaReFuL" rule: *sac, bonjour, neuf, mal*). Mind exceptions.

## Rhythm and intonation

- French is **syllable-timed**: syllables are of roughly equal length. There is no word stress; stress falls on the **last syllable of a rhythmic group** (lengthened and slightly raised).
- Liaison and enchaînement: consonants join the next vowel (*il est ici* → "i-lé-ti-ci"; *les amis* → /le.za.mi/).
- Intonation: within a sentence, groups rise (continuation); statements fall at the end; yes/no questions rise; wh-questions often fall.
- Avoid putting a stress accent on the first syllable, as in English.

## Common problems by native language

- **English speakers:** diphthongized vowels (*é* → "ay", *eau* → "oh-w"), aspirated /p t k/, English R, nasal vowels pronounced as vowel + N, stressing syllables inside words, reduced unstressed vowels, forgetting liaisons, and /y/ pronounced as /u/ or /ju/.
- **Swedish speakers:** the pitch accent and first-syllable stress carried into French, /ʁ/ replaced by Swedish /r/, nasal vowels replaced by oral vowel + /n/ or /ŋ/, and vowel length differences where French has none. Swedish /y/ and /ø/ transfer well.
- **Others:** ask the learner and adapt.

## Minimal pairs

rue / roue, tu / tout, vu / vous, dessus / dessous, dit / du, si / su, les / lait, beau / bon, pas / pan, mais / main, bon / bonne, peu / peur, jeune / jeûne, chat / sa, bon / pont.

## Practice sentences

- *Tu as vu la rue où vous êtes tous nés ?*
- *Un bon vin blanc, s'il vous plaît.*
- *Les amis arrivent à huit heures.*
- *Je veux deux œufs et du beurre.*
