# English (General American): Sound Guide

## Core target sounds

**Consonants**
- /ɹ/ (rhotic R): tongue tip curled back or bunched, no contact with the roof of the mouth, lips slightly rounded. Pronounce R after vowels too (*car*, *work*).
- /θ/ and /ð/ (*think*, *this*): tongue tip lightly between the teeth. /θ/ is voiceless, /ð/ is voiced.
- /w/ vs /v/: /w/ rounds the lips with no teeth contact (*west*). /v/ puts upper teeth on the lower lip (*vest*).
- /l/: tongue tip touches the ridge behind the upper teeth at the start of a word; the back of the tongue lifts for "dark L" at the end (*full*).
- /h/: breathy, no friction in the mouth (*hat*). Do not drop it.
- /z/ vs /s/: /z/ is voiced (*zoo*, *is*, *dogs*).
- Aspiration: /p t k/ at the start of stressed syllables carry a puff of air (*pin*, *time*, *car*). Hold a tissue in front of the mouth: it should move.
- Flap /ɾ/: /t/ or /d/ between vowels, after a stressed syllable, sounds like a quick D (*water*, *butter*, *city*).
- Final consonants: pronounce them clearly (*cab*, *cap*); vowels are longer before voiced endings (*bed* is longer than *bet*).

**Vowels**
- /iː/ vs /ɪ/ (*sheep* / *ship*): tense and long vs relaxed and short.
- /uː/ vs /ʊ/ (*fool* / *full*).
- /æ/ vs /ɛ/ vs /ʌ/ (*bad* / *bed* / *bud*): jaw wide for /æ/, mid for /ɛ/, relaxed and central for /ʌ/.
- /ɑ/ vs /ɔ/ vs /ʌ/ (*hot* / *hawk*, *hut*), depending on region; follow the learner's goal.
- Diphthongs /eɪ/ and /oʊ/ (*day*, *go*) glide; do not keep them pure.
- Schwa /ə/: the most common vowel. Unstressed syllables shrink to schwa (*banana* = /bəˈnænə/).

## Rhythm and intonation

- English is **stress-timed**: stressed syllables come at roughly even intervals; unstressed syllables are squeezed and shortened.
- Content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) are stressed. Function words (*to*, *of*, *and*, *can*) reduce: *I can go* = /aɪ kən ˈgoʊ/.
- Each sentence has a **focus word**: the last important content word gets the biggest pitch movement.
- Statements and wh-questions usually fall at the end. Yes/no questions usually rise.
- Connected speech: linking (*pick it up* = "pi-ki-tup"), reductions (*want to* → *wanna*, *going to* → *gonna*), and flapping.

## Common problems by native language

- **Swedish speakers:** /w/ as /v/; /z/ replaced by /s/; /ð/ and /θ/ replaced by /d/ or /t/ or /s/; monophthongal /eɪ/ and /oʊ/ (too pure); /ɹ/ tapped or trilled; /ʒ/ replaced by /ʃ/ or /ɧ/; lack of vowel reduction in unstressed syllables.
- **French speakers:** dropped /h/; /θ ð/ → /s z/ or /f v/; /ɪ/ → /iː/; /ʊ/ → /uː/; uvular R instead of /ɹ/; stress put on the final syllable of phrases rather than on the word's stressed syllable; no vowel reduction; pronouncing final syllables too clearly.
- **Others:** ask the learner and adapt.

## Minimal pairs

## Practice sentences (sound + rhythm)

- *Wally was really wearing a warm yellow rain jacket.*
- *I'd like a little bit of butter on my water roll, please.*
