# Swedish (Standard / Central Swedish): Sound Guide

## Core target sounds

**Vowels** (nine vowel letters, each with a **long** and a **short** form)
- Quantity rule: a stressed syllable is either **long vowel + short consonant** or **short vowel + long consonant**. *tak* /tɑːk/ (roof) vs *tack* /tak/ (thanks, with a longer K).
- Long vowels are pure, without glides: *se* /seː/, *bo* /buː/. English speakers tend to add an "ay" or "oh-w" glide; avoid it.
- /yː/ (*ny*, *by*): say /iː/ with tightly rounded lips. Contrast with /ʉː/ (*du*, *ful*): a rounded, forward, "in-between" sound (not English "oo"). Short /ɵ/ (*full*, *hund*) is related.
- /øː/ (*hör*) and /ɛː/ (*här*), /ɔ/ and /oː/ (*å*), /ɑː/ (*a*).
- Note that **short** Swedish vowels often differ in quality from long ones (*vila* vs *vill*).

**Consonants**
- /ɧ/ (*sj*, *sju*, *skjorta*, *stjärna*, *mission*): a "sh-like" sound produced with simultaneous friction at the palate and velum. Many learners get close with a strongly rounded "sh".
- /ɕ/ (*tj*, *kj*, *k* before front vowels: *tjugo*, *kjol*, *kärlek*): a soft "ch-like" /ç/, similar to a very light "sh" with the tongue raised toward the hard palate.
- /r/: tapped or trilled in Central Swedish (the tongue tip), uvular in the south. Either is understood.
- Retroflex consonants after /r/: *rt* = /ʈ/, *rd* = /ɖ/, *rn* = /ɳ/, *rl* = /ɭ/, *rs* = /ʂ/ (*kort*, *bord*, *barn*, *Karl*, *kors*). The R disappears into the following sound.
- /p t k/ are aspirated at the start of stressed syllables (as in English).
- /j/ (*ja*, *gjorde*) and /h/.

## Rhythm and intonation

- **Word stress** is lexical and usually on the first syllable of Germanic words; loanwords differ (*universiTET*).
- **Pitch accent**: every word has accent 1 (acute, one pitch peak) or accent 2 (grave, a fall and then a rise). It distinguishes words: *anden* (the duck, accent 1) vs *anden* (the spirit, accent 2). Practice by listening and imitating the "melody" before reading.
- Sentence intonation: statements fall at the end; questions rise, with sentence melody superimposed on word accents. The learner should not flatten the melody; Swedish speech is often described as "sing-song" to foreign ears.
- Swedish is **stress-timed**, like English, with a strong first-syllable stress.

## Common problems by native language

- **English speakers:** diphthongized long vowels, ignoring vowel and consonant quantity, English R (instead of a tap), /ʉː/ replaced by /uː/, /y/ replaced by /ju/ or /uː/, /ɧ/ replaced by "sh", and lack of pitch accent.
- **French speakers:** stress on the last syllable of the phrase, no quantity contrast, uvular R, nasal vowels replacing oral vowels, and lack of aspiration of /p t k/. The front rounded vowels transfer well.
- **Others:** ask the learner and adapt.

## Minimal pairs

tak / tack, mat / matt, ful / full, vila / vill, sy / si, ny / ni, hör / här, anden (duck) / anden (spirit).
